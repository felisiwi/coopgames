### Hub-wide
| # | Check | Rating | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First 30 seconds | weak | The HUD shows wind, speed and a trim hint (`src/hud.js:307-311`), but nothing on screen says which keys do what. Turning is A/D and trim is W/S, a non-obvious remap of WASD (`game.js:323-326`, `DESIGN.md:44-47`). Sail trim starts fully sheeted in (`game.js:204`, `trim: 0`), so the boat starts slow: seed 1 host does 1.61 m/s vs 6.43 m/s at ideal trim. Spawn headings are fixed east/west whatever the wind (`game.js:203`). Over 2000 random hub seeds, measured: the host spawns inside the no-go zone (speed 0) 13.1% of the time and the guest 12.4% (seeds 2, 3 and 777 put the guest at 0 m/s). In those spawns the HUD's "ease N°" hint does nothing. **Watch for:** a new player pressing W expecting to go forward; a player stuck at 0 kn at spawn. | Add a one-line key legend in the HUD column ("A/D steer · W/S sheet in/out · scroll zoom") that stays until the player has both turned and trimmed once. |
| 2 | Both players matter | fail | The only shared state is the wind, which the host owns (`game.js:209-256`). The other boat is just drawn (`game.js:340-349`). It never affects your speed, your view, your goals or what you can do. Trim isn't even synced (`game.js:344-346`). Take the guest away and the host's game is unchanged. This matches Felix's own "isn't really co-op yet". | Make P1's discoverables need both boats: a prop (e.g. a lighthouse lighting up, a jetty you can tie up at) only "completes" when both boats are within ~20 m of it at once. One player has to bring the other there. |
| 3 | A "come here" moment | fail | Nothing in the world prompts a call-out. There are no discoverables (`design-checks.md:17-20`), islands have no collision so you can't run aground (`heightAt` is never called in `game.js`; the HANDOVER claim checks out), and there are no events beyond wind shifts, which both players get at the same time. The partner's position never shows on the HUD or compass rose (`src/hud.js:329-330` draws only wind and own heading). Boats spawn 20 m apart (`config.js:66`, ±10 m), so "where are you?" won't come up at the start either. | Add a "ping" key that sends `{type:'ping', x, z}`. It drops a short-lived flare at your boat and puts a marker on the partner's compass rose: a cheap, cosy way to say "come look at this". |
| 4 | Graceful end | fail | There's no natural stopping point: no goal, no end state. On disconnect, the hub's "Connection lost" panel (`index.html:37-39`) sits inside `#lobby` (`index.html:15`), which is hidden during play (`index.html:142`). `showLost` only switches panels inside the lobby (`index.html:82-84,125-127`), so it never appears mid-game. The game can't see `onClose` (`index.html:155-157`) and doesn't check how old the partner's position is. `ts` is sent (`game.js:274`) but never read, so a departed partner's boat stays frozen on the water forever (`game.js:340-349`). | Treat the partner as gone if no `pos` message arrives for ~5 s: fade out and remove their boat, and show a small "your friend has left. Keep sailing, or close the tab" note. |
| 5 | Hub contract | pass | `game.json:2` `id: "windward"` matches the folder, with `name`, `description`, `players`, `owner` all present. `game.js:61` default-exports `start({ canvas, net, seed, role })`. It only imports three, `src/` and `shared/input.js` (`game.js:8-23`; no PeerJS, no `shared/net.js`). The model URL resolves via `import.meta.url` (`src/boat.js:25`). Same seed, same world, measured: `scatterIslands` gives identical output twice for seeds 1, 2, 3, 42 and 777 (97/104/98/104/98 islands), and `initialWind(seed)` is deterministic (`wind.test.js`). After that the host owns the wind, ordered by `seq` (`game.js:250-254`). Drop-in play: the other boat is created lazily on its first `pos` (`game.js:244-249`). The hub link-join itself wasn't exercised (headless only). | — |
| 6 | True to its own feel | weak | Lambert + `flatShading` everywhere the game builds its own meshes: water (`src/water.js:118-121`), islands and trees (`src/island.js:146,176-177`), buoys and rocks (`src/scatter.js:27,34,46`). Sail colours match ART (`game.js:190-191`). The water formula matches ART exactly (`src/water.js:65-66`). No sparkle, foam spray or reflections. **But** the boat is the vendored GLB loaded raw (`src/boat.js:46,58-66`). Its material is glTF `pbrMetallicRoughness` (read from `ship-small.glb`'s JSON), so GLTFLoader makes a `MeshStandardMaterial`, which ART's Materials rule bans. Its hull facets (~0.28 m, per HANDOVER) are far off the 4 m facet scale. ART's lighting numbers have also drifted from the code. ART says sun `0xffffff` @0.9, azimuth 55°, elevation 26°, hemisphere `0x89c4f4`/`0x1c4a70` @0.7, sky `0x89c4f4`. The code has `0xffd9a6` @1.4, azimuth 50°, elevation 10°, `0xcbc9ad`/`0x21406a` @0.45, and a sky gradient from `0xecf8f1` to `0x00faff` (`src/config.js:75-86`). The code matches ART's own "golden light", so ART's numbers are the stale side. | In `boat.js`'s `model.traverse`, replace each mesh's material with `MeshLambertMaterial({ map, color, flatShading: true })`, keeping the cloned and tinted sail. Separately, a human should refresh ART.md's Water and lighting numbers to match `config.js`. |

### Per-game (design-checks.md)
| Check | Rating | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sailing is a skill you can feel yourself getting better at | pass | The no-go zone is enforced: speed is 0 inside ±22.5° (`src/sail.js:39`, `sail.test.js`). At 20° off the wind, measured speed is 0. Trim changes speed a lot. Measured at full wind, ideal vs worst sheet: 45° off the wind 3.45 vs 0.86 m/s (4.0×), 90° off 13.30 vs 4.51 (2.95×), 135° off 12.86 vs 3.22 (4.0×), 170° off 11.32 vs 2.83 (4.0×). Tacking costs something real: a 45°→45° tack at 75% wind takes 0.67 s and loses 85% of speed at the low point (simulated with `game.js`'s `approachSpeed` and `CONFIG`). The HUD shows the effect: hint text plus a trim bar using the same `TRIM_FULL_WINDOW_DEG` as the speed model (`src/hud.js:259-299`), and speed in knots. **Watch for (feel):** whether 135°/s turning (`config.js:14`) makes tacks feel twitchy rather than weighty, and whether players chase the trim bar instead of watching the boat. | — |
| The sea tells you about the wind | weak | The amplitude and speed formulas match ART exactly (`src/water.js:65-66`). Wave crests run along the wind bearing (`src/water.js:69`), and a world-space arrow sits over the boat (`src/windArrow.js:143-152`). So wind direction is readable without the compass, in principle. But the range is squeezed. Wind strength never drops below 0.4 (`src/wind.js:34`; initial wind 0.5 at least, `src/wind.js:24`). Measured max wave height: 0.227 m at strength 0.4 vs 0.397 m at 1.0, only 1.75× apart. So ART's "calm = glassy" never happens in play. Whether ±0.4 m waves can be seen at all from 125 m away (`config.js:52`) is for a playtest. **Watch for:** can a player say "wind's picking up" from the water before reading the HUD? | Let wind targets drop to near-calm: change `nextWindTarget` strength to `0.1 + Math.random() * 0.9` (`src/wind.js:34`). The sea then goes visibly glassy in lulls, and lulls themselves become something to read. |
| Going somewhere is worth it | fail | As the check predicted. Islands are just scenery: no props, no discoverables (`design-checks.md:17-20`), and no collision or grounding (`game.js` never samples `heightAt`), so you can sail straight through them. Arriving at an island changes nothing. Islands do differ in size (seed 1: 1 starter, 3 large, 10 medium, 83 skerries) and coastline (`island.test.js`), but nothing rewards visiting one. | Build P1 with a discovery rule from day one: each prop is logged ("found 3/12") when a boat comes within reach, shown in the HUD for both players. That also lets this check get its own serendipity measurement. |

### Most important next step
Fix **Both players matter** (check 2), because without it the other fixes don't matter much. Today Windward is two people sailing next to each other: nothing one player does changes anything for the other, so it's the "screensaver with a chat window" the hub checks warn against, and it fits Felix's own read that it "isn't really co-op yet". The cheapest real fix fits the cosy, keep-your-own-boat direction and arrives with P1, which is already planned. Make the props things you find *together*: one only counts as found when both boats are near it at once. That one rule gives each player a reason to care where the other is (check 2), a reason to say "come over here" (check 3, made easy by the ping), and a reason to sail somewhere (third per-game check). Pair it with the stale-partner timeout (check 4) so that when one person leaves, the other isn't left with a frozen boat.
