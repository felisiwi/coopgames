# People and details of 1950 (research for the story)

Research for the 1950 home base: the Måseskär keepers, what an AGA gas-light service looked like, boatbuilding around Skaftö, a fishing girl's
life, and the exact light at Kråksundsgap Södra. Machine-readable twin: `data/people-1950.json` (sources + items, flagged). Builds on
`docs/WORLD-1950.md` (cited as "WORLD-1950 S02" etc.). Read 2026-11-06. New sources: A01-A27 (lights) and B01-B39 (people).

**How to read the flags.** `documented` = a source I read says it. `derived` = our arithmetic. `inferred` = reasoned from documented facts.
`INVENTED` / `INVENTED-DIALOGUE` = a suggestion for the game, never history; each says which real source it is grounded in. "search-summary"
= only a search snippet was seen: weak. Real named people appear only where a source names them; no boat, yard or keeper name is guessed.
The two drafts were written by research agents; **I re-read these myself against their sources:** the Gullholmen cannery dates (Prisma VG: rebuilt

## What this changes in the design (read this first)

1. **The "light from the future" at Kråksundsgap Södra cannot be a light where nothing stands.** An unmanned AGA light burned there in 1950: an
   automatic light on Bråtö dates from 1911 (octagonal iron lantern), exchanged in 1941 (fyrwiki: the station "established" 1941; Bx(2) WRG 7 s 8 M),
   and only the 13 m concrete tower with its octagonal lantern is from 1954 (built "near the water"; its sector division is from 1954). What really
   changed in 1954 is the *tower*, not the existence of a light; the 1950 flash is probably a two-flash group almost identical to today's Fl(2) WRG 6 s.
   Honest options (section 5): (a) keep Södra as the strange place but make the "future" part the tall white tower and the new sector split, in place of
   the small iron lantern; (b) use a light truly absent in 1950 inside the window: **Släggabåden (1961)** or the sea towers **Brandskärsflak / Dynabrott (1974)**;
   (c) the existing fallback, Måseskär's fixed red of 1865-1887. A nice true detail for (a): the 1941 iron lantern still exists, restored, in a roundabout in Lysekil.
2. **Måseskär: no named 1950 keeper exists in anything read.** Staff were three families (fyrmästare, fyrvaktare, fyrbiträde) plus a summer relief. Names are
   documented for other years (Alfred Nilén 1912-25, Holm 1924, Anders Danielsson 1901-12, Gunnar Lundberg 1960s): the game keeper must be unnamed or invented.
   Also: **no documented Bohuslän ghost-ship or phantom-light tale was found**; the suggested lines rest on storm-petrel lore ("stormtrollet": carries drowned
   sailors' souls, drawn to the light), lyktgubbe lore and a keeper's maxim, and are labelled INVENTED-DIALOGUE.
3. **The relight is real work, serviced from Erholmen, not locally.** The manned gas station on Erholmen (SW of Marstrand, 1918-1965) filled the tubes and serviced
   about 150 unmanned lights by the early 1950s from a work yacht, with a rowing eka for landing, tubes of ~100-120 kg carried on stretchers; a trip took 2-3 weeks.
   Section 2 has a 12-step relight sequence with each step marked documented or INVENTED. Sound: only the flasher's "pop" is documented (and only in a 1955 US manual for the
   same apparatus); the smell is faint and garlic-like; hiss and tick are not documented.
4. **For Svalan (a clinker koster) do not put "caulk the planking" on the job list.** Driving yarn into seams ("driva") is for carvel hulls and decks; a clinker hull is riveted and
   tight at the lands. No Skaftö, Fiskebäckskil or Grundsund boatyard is named in any source found (Orust is the nearest sourced model). Svalan is ~8 m; the documented koster fishing
   original is 10-12 m: a rebuilt older sailing koster is plausible.
5. **Britta has probably just left school** (derived: folkskola was 7 years in the 1930s; a girl born 1936 starting at 7 finishes about spring 1950). The Gullholmen cannery was
   rebuilt in 1950-52 and ran to 1965, so **it was operating in 1950**: this resolves the "closed after WWI" conflict noted in WORLD-1950 (the Länsstyrelsen text loses to the Prisma VG exhibit).
6. Smaller corrections: Dalén's blinding accident is dated 1912 by fyr.org (the brief said 1913); Måseskär's three-flash pattern probably dates from 1887, though one note says
   1950; the pilot-school end is 1910 (fyr.org) vs 1917 (Bohusläns museum); the Sandö record conflicts (see section 3); "Fiskebäcks Båtvarv" in a koster register is in Gothenburg.

## 1. Måseskär in 1950: the keepers' life

### Facts (sourced)

Staff and housing
- Staff in 1943-44: fyrmästare (Fm), fyrvaktare (Fv), fyrbiträde (Fb), plus a summer relief (sem.-vik.) for the whole holiday period; station class I. [A01, "Våra fyrar 1944"]
- Dwellings in 1944: Fm 3 rooms and kitchen; Fv and Fb 2 rooms and kitchen each; "inga bekvämligheter" (no amenities). [A01] One building held three separate homes; wash-and-bake house, sheds and a wall made a sheltered courtyard. [A21]
- Island about 500 x 300 m, no notable vegetation. [A01] Last family left in 1969; after that only staff on work weeks; de-manned 1997. [A21]
- Fyrmästare named in sources: **Alfred Nilén** (1912-1925, real person; pictured with the mist cannon 1919) and **Holm** (pictured 1924) [A01, A19]; **Anders Danielsson** (1901-1912, real person, see below) [A19]; **Gunnar Lundberg** (fyrmästare in the 1960s, repaired clocks as a hobby and locals brought floor and wall clocks out to him) [A01 via search summary of fyrwiki, A22]. **No source read names anyone serving in 1950.** fyr.org keeps a personnel database (fyr.org/fyrpersonal, link on the Måseskär page) that I did not query.

Routines
- Keepers filled and trimmed the lamp, polished the lens, wound the weight that turned the lens (weight about 115-116 kg on a 40 m line), kept the fog-signal machinery ready at all times, maintained buildings, piers and boats, and wrote weather observations. [A21, A01]
- In 1944 the station had no storm-warning station but kept weather observations in a journal. [A01]
- Light until 1950: three-flame paraffin Lux mantle lamp (1909; 60,000 Hefner). 1949: special ball bearings in the lens (1,695 kr). **1950: cable laid, lamp electrified (280,000 Hefner), engine house rebuilt, siren replaced by a tyfon (two blasts per 30 s).** 1953 new fog-signal keyer and boat crane; 1955 (or 1957 per a stencil note) the weight and clockwork replaced by an electric motor. [A01]
- Fog signal history: gong 1865, two 6-pounder cannons 1873, steam siren 1883/1885, compressed-air siren 1916/1924, tyfon 1950; one cannon saved in 1927 by a staff white lie about its weight ("on account of its colossal weight the job was postponed"); it still stands there. [A01]
- Ring-line telephone between dwelling and fog station since 1928. [A01] The path down to the fog-signal house was narrow and hard in darkness or fog (1950s photo). [A21]

Supplies, boats, mail, school
- 1944: 4 trips a month April-September, 3 trips October-March, to Käringön with the service's (Lotsverket's) motor boat; post office, shop and folkskola all on Käringön, about 2 nautical miles away. Drinking water from a 42 m drilled well (1942). Fuel transport not free. [A01, "Våra fyrar 1944"]
- A keeper's child remembers Christmas week 1941 (wartime ration): the station's motor boat could be used only once a week; the week's goods had to last until weather allowed more; letters, post and fresh food were welcome; newspapers could be over a week old and were "our cinema and theatre"; a battery radio was reserved mainly for the weather report; they rowed home from Käringön "in the three-flash light of Måseskär". [A18, Lars Wässing, real person]
- Men usually fetched the mail and provisions and could also fish; women ran the household and were the most tied to the place. [A22]
- Vegetable plots in small round stone-walled beds filled with soil brought from the mainland (potatoes, vegetables); fish when weather allowed; dry goods stored in advance. [A21, A22]
- Radio was named by elders as the most important change; a telephone later helped. [A22]
- School: the pilot-children's school (lotsbarnsskola) ran in an attic room, which was also the teacher's room. fyrwiki: 1881-1910 (gap 1893-1902). 1944: pupils go to the Käringön folkskola. [A01]

Hardships and safety
- Isolation; weather decided every trip; no regular ferry. [A21, A22]
- Keepers sometimes took part in sea rescue. [A22] (No specific Måseskär rescue found.)
- **Fyrmästare Anders Danielsson (real, 1901-12) drowned in June 1912 when his boat capsized off Käringön on the way home from a fishing trip; his 13-year-old son was rescued.** [A19, quoting Bohusläningen 25 June 1912]
- Families in the 1900s: 15 people lived on the island; staff often came from Käringön and Ödsmål families. [A21, A19]

Character colour (all documented, mostly for other years)
- The 1927 cannon white lie. [A01]
- Storm petrels ("sjöpink" / "stormtrollet" in Käringön speech) are caught and ringed at Måseskär more than anywhere else on the west coast (49 of 85 birds in a 25-year project). Folk belief: they warn of storm and carry dead sailors' souls; they are drawn to lighthouse lights at night. [A20]
- Comparison only (other station, Väderöbod): families wrote shopping lists for the service boat's four land trips a month; butter in crocks under brine; eggs in water glass; 1921-22 ice winter poem by the teacher. [A24]

### Uncertain / conflicting
- **Pilot school end:** fyrwiki says 1881-1910; Bohusläns museum says the school closed in 1917. [A01 vs A21]
- **Steam siren:** 1883 or 1885 (fyrwiki gives both). **Clockwork to electric motor:** 1955 or 1957. [A01]
- **Light characteristic:** fyrwiki says Bx(3) W 30 s since 1887 ("3 blänkar var 30:e sekund"), but a 1940/50s stencil note says the characteristic was changed to three flashes per 30 s in 1950 together with electrification. Lars Wässing's 1941 "three-flash" memory supports the earlier date. [A01, A18] The WORLD-1950 data (Bx(3) W 30 s) stays correct for 1950 either way.
- Tower height: fyrwiki 21.7-22 m, ibiblio 27 m (not resolved here; WORLD-1950 uses 22 m).

- How often supplies came in 1950 (only 1944 figures and a 1941 anecdote); who delivered coal/paraffin and how.
- Daily watch schedule (hours), whether the keepers watched the light all night after electrification (the lamp was monitorable from the dwelling by 1955 per WORLD-1950 S02).
- Animals kept (none documented).

## 2. AGA gas lights in practice (the relight moment)

### Facts (sourced)

The system
- AGA light = acetylene burning in an open flame behind a lens; automatic, needing no staff. Depending on the gas store, a visit was needed about once or twice a year (fyrwiki); the main parts: pressure regulator, flasher valve (klippventil), two-jet burner, a permanent pilot flame (evighetslåga), and the sun valve. [A11, A13]
- **Flasher (klippventil, Dalén 1905):** gas enters a chamber closed by a lever armature; a leather diaphragm is pushed out by gas pressure, opens the outlet, a spring pushes the gas out through the burner, the diaphragm drops back and the lever closes the outlet; the cycle repeats. Purely mechanical, driven by gas pressure. Character is changed by swapping aluminium filler pieces or a ratchet wheel (Fl(2), Fl(3)). Gas use of a Fl 3 s light (0.3 s flash) is 10 % of a continuously lit flame. [A13]
- **Burner:** two-jet burner; the two gas jets collide, mix with air, and burn as a clear "fish-tail" flame without soot, lit by the pilot. [A13, A11]
- **Sun valve (solventil, 1907):** several equal rods of the same metal, one dull black and the rest bright, standing parallel; light heats the black rod more so it lengthens a little; a lever closes the gas valve by day; when the light fades the rods equalise and a spring valve opens and the light starts. Insensitive to air temperature because all rods expand equally; the valve seat is magnetised to give a clear open/closed snap. Saves up to 93 % of the gas together with the flasher. First installed on Furuholmen in 1907. [A12, A16] The pilot stays lit by day. [A11]
- **Mantle (Dalén) lights:** a separate, brighter type: acetylene and air burn inside an incandescent mantle (glödstrumpa); an automatic changer feeds a new mantle if the old one breaks. [A11, A10] Which of Hättan, Gäven, Grötö, Oxskär were mantle or open-flame is **not found**.
- Gas in cylinders (gasackumulatorer / "tuber"): steel shells filled with porous AGA-massa soaked in acetone, charged at 12-15 bar; AK-25 weighs 61 kg and AK-50 118 kg and these were the usual lighthouse sizes. [A14] Erholmen memoir: each tube about 120 kg including 9 kg of gas, 1 to 12 tubes per light by brightness and character. [A09, A08] Northern Bohuslän supervisor: two batteries of six tubes each, "nearly 100 kg" apiece. [A10]

A service visit
- From 1918 to 1965 the manned gas station on **Erholmen** (SW of Marstrand; fyrmästare, fyrvaktare and two fyrbiträden) made the acetylene, filled the tubes and serviced the unmanned lights of the Västra Lotsdistriktet (18 lights at the start, "just over 150" by the early 1950s). [A08, A09] Gäven specifically: after 1920 Erholmen staff changed the tubes. [A25]
- Work boat: the "Erholmsjakten" (a 52-foot yacht from Donsö yard, early 1920s, spartan: four bunks, kerosene stoves). Tubes in the hold; at the light they were transferred to a big rowing boat (eka) towed behind and rowed ashore. In rough water they were landed far from the light and carried on stretchers (bår) over the rocks. A trip could take 2-3 weeks. [A08, A09]
- Memoir of Lennart Hermansson (real person, worked at Erholmen 1946-51 with a break for service 1949-50): lights also needed lime and coal-tar painting, the sun valves had to be adjusted at intervals, rust chipping and painting were part of the job; no boots, rainwear, life belts or life jackets were issued; up to 120 empty tubes came home at the end of a trip. The lime slurry left over from making acetylene was used to whitewash the lights. [A09, A08]
- In northern Bohuslän a local fisherman, **Anker Andersson** (real, 1902-1982, supervisor for lights off Resö from 1 Feb 1937 to about 1970), changed tubes every second, maybe third, year and checked in between; if a light went out he went out and relit it. Causes he names: the pilot flame had gone out, or all mantles in the automatic changer were used up. The boat was a big rowing eka towed by his fishing boat or snipa; landing at Väcker and Ramskär needed near-calm and no old swell; he hired one or two helpers at hourly pay; he describes tubes lowered from the lantern gallery on hooks. [A10]
- Sound, smell, look: the 1955 US Coast Guard manual (same AGA apparatus) says a light "pop" can be heard each time the flasher functions. [A17] Acetylene is nearly odourless with a faint garlic-like smell. [A15] A correctly set pilot shows a large blue flame; yellow means soot. [A17] Hissing, ticking and clicking are **not documented** in any source I read.

Checking and relighting (US manual, 1955, for the same equipment; Swedish practice not documented in detail)
- Remove cylinders before they are fully empty; renew at about 2 atmospheres. Close the line shut-off valve and each cylinder valve, unscrew the pipe fittings on the empties, fit blind plugs and caps. [A17, p.20-87]
- Hoist or lower A-50 cylinders with a special lifting cap or a sling, never a rope on the valve; do not drop cylinders onto each other in the boat. [A17]
- Connect each full cylinder: centre the steel nipple, hand-start the brass nut, tighten with the wrench but not too hard (soft aluminium washer, renew it each time); record serial numbers. Then shut off, fit a test gauge, open the valve gently, read the pressure, test for leaks. [A17]
- Outage causes: wind blew the flame out (check for cracked panes; **ventilate the lantern before relighting**); pilot carbonised (sooty lens, yellow flame); defective flasher; empty cylinders. Pilot check: it is big and blue; in daylight cup your hand over it or view through a tube; a pilot that is too high carbonises and smokes up the panes. [A17, p.20-83] Swedish detail: hold a match over the pilot in daylight; if the match ignites, the pilot works; do not let it burn yellow. [A13]
- Flasher set-up: check the characteristic by timing; do not try internal repairs in the field, swap the unit. [A17]
- Hazards: acetylene may explode spontaneously from 3 bar; explosive range 2-82 % in air; cylinders can burst in a fire; copper, silver and brass over 70 % copper must not touch it. [A15] At Erholmen two bad explosions in the early 1920s (a man slightly hurt; one man lost a thumb to silver nitrate). [A09] Slippery skerries, swell, heavy loads; "does not know if Arbetsmiljöverket would allow it today". [A10]
- Gas bought from AGA after 1965 sooted the flame and put lights out. [A08, A09] (Outside the 1950 window.)

Dalén in a few sourced facts
- Gustaf Dalén (1869-1937); AGA-massa 1906; flasher valve 1905; sun valve 1907; the German patent office doubted the sun valve until he demonstrated it by drawing a dark curtain over the window and the flame lit. [A16]
- Nobel Prize in Physics **1912** (fyrwiki; I could not open nobelprize.org, 403). [A16]
- Accident: fyrwiki says a serious explosion accident in **1912** cost him his sight, and he stayed optimistic ("VAR OPTIMIST" pins). **The brief said 1913; the one source read says 1912.** [A16]

- Tube weight: 120 kg with 9 kg gas [A08, A09] vs about 100 kg [A10] vs AK-25 61 kg / AK-50 118 kg [A14]. The sizes may differ by light.

### The relight sequence the player would do
D = documented in sources (above); I = INVENTED simplification.
1. D: Land from the rowing boat in calm water, ideally no swell; if not calm, land the tubes far off and carry them. [A10, A09]
2. D: Carry the cylinders on a stretcher up the rock; two people. (Game: one person, short carry.) I for the simplification.
3. D: Close the gas shut-off to the lantern and the old cylinder valves; unscrew the pipe fittings; fit blind plugs and caps. [A17]
4. D: Swing out the empties and hook the full ones on the gallery line (Hättan-type small lights probably lack a hoist: I). [A10, A17]
5. D: Connect each full cylinder: fresh washer, hand-start the brass nut, then the wrench. [A17]
6. D: Open the cylinder valve slowly and check for a leak/gauge reading. (Game: smell/hiss cue is I; gas smell is documented only as faint garlic [A15].)
7. D: Ventilate the lantern, then check the pilot flame: large and blue, no yellow; in daylight cup your hand or hold a match over it. [A17, A13] If out, relight it (the lighting action itself is inferred, I).
8. D: Check that the sun valve is working: it closes by day and opens by itself when it darkens. [A12] (Game: cover/uncover the black rod to test is I.)
9. D: Wait for dusk; the flasher "pops" and the light starts its pattern; count the flashes to confirm the characteristic. [A17, A13]
10. D: Put caps and blind plugs neatly at the base of the cylinder bank; note serial numbers. [A17]
11. I: Burnt-out mantle: a mantle-changer light would need a new mantle fed in (documented that such a machine existed, [A10]); simplify to "replace the broken mantle".
12. I: Wipe the lantern panes and lens (cleaning is documented as part of Erholmen maintenance only in the form of painting, not lens cleaning).

## 3. Boatbuilding on Skaftö, and repairing a storm-damaged koster

### Facts (sourced)
- No Skaftö yard named. The only yard name in Fiskebäckskil is Lyckans Slip, today a marina/varv with no history in the source [B34, B29]. null for named yards. (P01)
- Big Grundsund-registered fishing boats came from Orust/Tjörn: the wooden Sandö LL 158, launched 1948 at V & T Löfbergs Slip och Båtvarv, Rönnäng (Tore and Viktor Löfberg; real firm and people, source Bohusläns museum): 24.6 x 6.6 m, carvel oak frames and planks, copper sheathing in places, oiled Norrland-pine deck, 225 hp June Munktell (replaced 1955 by a B&W Alpha 330 hp) [B15, WORLD-1950 S48]. (P02)
- Nearest sourced model of a family yard is Orust: Kungsviken had about 12 yards at once in the 1950s (jullar, snipor, Andungar, Folkbåtar) [B19]; yards in barns, men farmers in summer and builders in winter, wife and seven children at home [B18]. Real example in the manuscript: Bröderna Martinssons Båtvarv, Svineviken, started 1943 (29 Långedrag jollar 1943-53); Holger Johansson, started at 13, could build two ekor a week or a motor jolle in a month; Bröderna Jacobsson, Dragsmark, with seven children in the family (Carl started the Munkeby yard 1942) [B18]. These are Orust/Dragsmark, NOT Skaftö. (P03)
- Builder's child: home from school, straight down to the yard; children were used as counter-hold when riveting (iron cone "skam"), which they found boring and ran from [B18]. (P04)
- Post-war: huge demand because motor boats had lain ashore through the war; brothers sometimes worked 16 hours a day; J10 jolle sold at 1,225 kr incl. sails (war years); ironwork froze to the hand outdoors at Donsö in the 1940s [B18]. (P05)
- Planking: boards marked with moulds ("mallas") and sawn, steamed in a steam box, handled hot and wet with gloves, clamped, wedge ("trätving") driven in; the travelling plank-fitter ("bordfyllare") with the axe "Hjärtumyxan", used the axe, never a saw [B18]. (P06)
- Clinker order: keel and stems, planking from the plank next to the keel upward, frames fitted after the planking (steam-bent oak/ash or hewn/sawn); older clinker boats built with no moulds or only a midship frame; carvel boats framed first [B21, B23, B35]. Fastening: treenails with nails/screws; later copper nails with roves riveted over [B23, B35]. (P07)
- Koster: 10-12 m fishing original, clinker, gaff rig with bowsprit, decked drift-net boat of the 1800s [B20, WORLD-1950 S50]; fishermen who got hot-bulb engines c.1900 ordered new kosters with wider sterns, and city workers bought the old sailing kosters and rebuilt them with deck and deckhouse [B18]. Named koster builders in Wikipedia (Tomas i Kile, Carl Stjern, J.H. Seldén etc.) are not Skaftö [B20]. (P08, P09)
- A koster built privately around 1949-50: "Kennedy", 7.20 x 2.52 m, 25 m2 sail, clinker, A. Laurin design, built by Holger Eliasson, hull started at Färjenäs and finished in his garden [B32]. WARNING: "Fiskebäcks Båtvarv" in that register is in Gothenburg, not Fiskebäckskil. (P10)
- Engines: Skandia hot-bulb engines from Lysekil (1899 Lars Laurin and Karl Forsström; named Skandiaverken 1920; hot-bulb to 1966; diesel from 1933); fishermen first feared the noise [B25]. Skaftö.com (single tourism source) says Laurin was a Grundsund fisherman's son [B29]. June Munktell in Sandö [B15]; Bolinder named at Hållö [WORLD-1950 S03]. (P11)
- Materials: oak (frames/planking on big carvel boats), oiled Norrland pine decks [B15]; frames oak, elm, ash [B23]. Paint: Sandö white linseed paint above waterline, red stripe, red bottom [B15]; linseed paint dries about two days, alkyd arrived in the 1930s and spread in the 1950s [B24]. (P12, P13)
- Tools: trollpacka (angle board with pendulum, checks each plank bevel; Gösta Johansson's yard, Kungsviken, photographed 1978) [B16]; shores and clamps [B35]; caulking irons, mallet, hook, pitch pot [B22]; adze for fitting frames [B23]. (P18)
- Launching: small boats by horse on an unshod sledge, larger on a cradle rolled on logs [B18]. (P19)

### Uncertain / conflicting
- Sandö: the Bohusläns museum record read says built 1948, home port Grundsund; another museum record seen only as a snippet says it was built for Klädesholmen and was bought by Grundsund fishermen from Käringön in 1972 (and gives "1960") [B15]. Home-port claim in WORLD-1950 therefore uncertain.
- Lars Laurin's Grundsund origin is single-source tourism [B29].
- The Orust yard stories are from a local-history manuscript with no named author in the file [B18] (secondary). "Källvikens Varv sett från Skaftöbron" appears in it, but that is a different place from our Skaftö as far as the text goes; not used.
- Caulking: documented for carvel hulls and decks; a clinker koster is normally not caulked between lands [B22, B21]. This contradicts any "driva the planking" step.
- Larch: not found in any source.

### Repairing a storm-damaged koster: the steps
Documentation level in brackets. The sequence is an INVENTED simplification for the game; individual techniques are documented where marked.
1. Survey where she lay: probe planks, frames, keel and stem; decide what to save. [INVENTED; general cut-out-and-replace principle only in a weak snippet, B39] (R01)

## 4. Fishing families on Skaftö in 1950 (for Britta)

### Facts (sourced)
- School: compulsory seven years in the 1930s, eight years in the 1950s; enhetsskola trials 1949/50 [B26]. By our arithmetic a girl born 1936 starting at 7 finishes folkskola about spring 1950 (derived, uncertain). (Q01)
- Cannery work: at Gullholmen girls started after confirmation as "småtöser" (helper work such as laying on bay leaf and onion) and later could work piece rate as "styckare"; women gutted anchovy and cut herring, "inläggerskor" filled and sealed tins; men moved goods and made tin cans [B01]. The Gullholmen factory (J.J. Hallgren, later AB Fyrtornet) was rebuilt 1938-39 and 1950-52, renamed AB Fyrtornet in the 1950s, merged 1962, closed 1965 [B01]: operating in 1950. Hälleviksstrand cannery 1934-1976 [WORLD-1950 S39]; Grundsund had a Bovik mackerel cannery [B27]. (Q02, Q03)
- Household (Bernhardson, born Grundsund 1915; father fisherman on his own cutter "Welfare", mother a farmer's daughter): families lived from sea and smallholding; women carried large families, fuel and water shortages and heavy farm and fishing work; men often away; widows helped mostly by other women; winter evenings of stories about hardship, shipwrecks, the supernatural [B03, B04]. (Q04)
- Water carried with a yoke and buckets with "skvalpeträ"; six or seven well-carrying traditions on Skaftö; a 1930s scene [B08]. (Q05)
- Play: "rutebåt" on a rolled-in salt tub, 1920s scene [B07]. (Q06)
- Lore: meeting a woman on the way to fishing was very unlucky [B05]; ice-crack omen on Skaftö in the 1940s [B11]; ghost voices in a dead calm "blegg" then storm [B12]. (Q07)
- War years: wreck fat hoisted into the sjöbod under rationing and blockade [B09]; coal hauled from a wreck over the ice in a fuel shortage [B10]. (Q08)
- Winters: nets frozen in the net box, Skaftö 1945 [B06]; ice fisherman 1947 [B04]; real fisherman Erik Benjaminsson (1904-1963), Islandsberg, drowned 1963 in ice [B14]. (Q09)
- Summer guests: bath resort since the late 1800s, mainly a summer resort in the 1900s [B28]; skerry pensions for children's sea air in the 1920s; Rågårdsviks Pensionat opened 1926 [B30]. (Q10)

## 5. Kråksundsgap Södra (the "light from the future")

### Facts (sourced)

Where
- Fyrwiki gives **58°06.1'N 11°26.5'E** for the Södra station (= 58.1017 N, 11.4417 E; derived); Felix's pin is 58.1006 N, 11.4405 E, about 140 m away (derived). [A03]
- ibiblio: northwest point of the island of **Bråtö**, about 1.6 km south of Hälleviksstrand, boat access only; the gap lies between Högholmen and Bråtö. [A07] Kråksundsgap is about 2.5 nm ESE of Käringön, inside Måseskär (so the Måseskär light is in view from this coast). [A02]

History 1890-1954 (the exact record)
- **1890-1911:** manned station on Högholmen (combined light and dwelling; a fixed white light with red sector, 18 m focal height); two keepers' children and wives lived there; 4 keepers in total; Kråksundsgap Övre (1896, a lamp on a trestle) and Tenholmen were also tended. [A05, A02]
- **1911:** the station was de-manned (last keeper Niklas Bergman served only 15 days, 8-23 Aug 1911). **An octagonal iron fyrkur with automatic AGA light was built on Högholmen; "at the same time" another octagonal iron fyrkur with AGA light was built on Bråtö, later called Kråksundsgap Södra; and an AGA light replaced the Övre.** [A05]
- **1941:** the Bråtö lantern "was exchanged for another" and is from then on called Kråksundsgap Södra [A05]. A photo caption in the Lysekil association's article says the old iron lantern "had been in use since **1 November 1941**" [A06]. The same year the Högholmen lantern was removed and replaced by a white concrete daymark board; the Övre lantern was removed and its base became a white cairn (kummel); Kråksundsgap Norra was added on the north side (Blänket) [A05] (fyrwiki main page says Norra came in 1956 [A02]; see conflicts).
- **1954:** "a 13 m high concrete tower with octagonal lantern was built **near the water** on Södra Kråksundsgap and replaced the former lantern" [A02, A05]. The same year the light got "its present sector division" [A03]. A Bohusläns museum photo from 1954 shows the lighthouse and a kummel [A02 caption].
- **1956:** Kråksundsgap Norra (6th-order, AGA, Sx W 90/min, 4 M, 36 cd, 4.5 m) built on a white storage hut (per fyrwiki main page) [A02].
- **1968:** white concrete tower 13 m with black conical base, 4th-order lens (500 mm), AGA, Bx(2) WRG 6 s 8 M; intensities W 480 cd, R 120 cd, G 60 cd. [A02]
- **199?:** solar power; the AGA unit was taken down. [A02, A03]
- **About 2010:** the octagonal lantern was replaced with a glass-fibre-reinforced plastic one; the old lantern went to Lysekil municipality and now stands as the "roundabout lighthouse" in the Dalskogen roundabout at Lysekil's entrance. [A03] Lysekils Vänner restored it; the article calls it the old iron light, in use since 1 Nov 1941 and fitted with new plastic panes and a lantern top. [A06]

### Uncertain / conflicting

### What this means for the design (INVENTED, grounded in the above)
- "A light burns where no lighthouse stands yet" **cannot be done at Kråksundsgap Södra.** Options:
  1. Keep Södra as the strange place, but make the "future" part something that really did change: a **tall white concrete tower** and a different two-flash pattern **in place of the small iron lantern and the 1950 pattern**. Honest framing: the keeper says the old light now looks different, not that nothing was there.
  2. Use a light that really was absent in 1950 inside the window: **Släggabåden** (established 1961, WORLD-1950 S15) or **Brandskärsflak / Dynabrott** (sea towers, 1974, ibiblio via LIGHTHOUSES.md). These are the documented "nothing there yet" candidates.

## The best story details (sourced)

### From the lights research
1. Christmas week 1941 at Måseskär: wartime ration, the station's motor boat allowed once a week, newspapers a week old as "our cinema", the battery radio kept for the weather report, and a forgotten Christmas sausage fetched by rowing around the "Pikarne" holmes, home by the three-flash light. [A18]
2. A burnt-out Dalén mantle let the flame race straight through and ignite a wooden stick that kept the changer locked; when the stick burned off, a fresh mantle was fed in. Anker Andersson's son watched this as a boy at each tube change. [A10]
3. Tubes of about 120 kg were carried on stretchers over rocks and "deep valleys"; no life jackets issued; the lime slurry left over from making acetylene was used to whitewash the lights. [A09, A08]
4. Testing a pilot flame in daylight by holding a match over it, and the flasher's "pop" on each flash; a yellow flame means soot and a dark light. [A13, A17]
5. The 1927 order to move Måseskär's last mist cannon to Vinga was stalled by the staff's white lie about its weight; the cannon is still on the island. [A01]
6. Kråksundsgap Södra's 1941 iron lantern now sits in a Lysekil roundabout, restored by Lysekils Vänner. [A03, A06]
7. Fyrmästare Anders Danielsson of Måseskär drowned in 1912 on a fishing trip home to Käringön; his 13-year-old son was saved. [A19]
8. Dalén convinced the German patent office by pulling a dark curtain over a daylit window: the flame lit. [A16]

### From the people research
1. The wreck's 300 kg of fine fat hoisted into the boathouse under rationing, "the sea gave too" [B09].
2. Children rolling a salt tub into the sea and using it as a ferry between piers, to the mothers' despair [B07].
3. Meeting a woman on the way to fishing is unlucky, but not on the way home with full baskets [B05].
