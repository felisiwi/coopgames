# The world around 1950

Data and notes for the coast in the game's start year, Epoch VI "Summer guests". Machine-readable twin:
`data/world-1950.json` (source ids S01... are in its `sourceRegistry`). Region: 58.085-58.30 N, 11.25-11.50 E.
Every page, PDF and image listed was read on 2026-11-06; "search summary" marks something seen only as a
search-engine snippet (weak, never used as the sole basis for a fact).

**How to read the flags.** `uncertain` = not directly documented for 1950 (estimated, inferred, from a nearby
year, from a weak source, or the sources disagree). `invented` = our recommendation, no source says it existed.
Confidence: documented (a source read states it for the place at or near 1950), derived (our arithmetic),
inferred (reasoned from related documented facts), invented. Names, dates, counts and colours no source gave
are `null` plus a line in `notFound`.

## What the research found, in one screen

- **Keepers.** In the game window **only Måseskär had lighthouse keepers in 1950** (fyrmästare, fyrvaktare,
  fyrbiträde; manned 1865-1997). Hållö (15 km north, outside the window) also had keepers (to 1969). Everything
  else (Islandsberg, Hättan, Gäven, Fredagsholmen, Grötö, Stångholmen, Oxskär ...) was an **unmanned
  AGA/Dalén automatic light**. The design note "most still have keepers" is wrong for this coast.
- **Islandsberg was not dark.** The old keeper's-house light went dark in 1938 and the station was de-manned,
  but a new automatic concrete tower 425 m north **was lit** (Bx(4) WRG 12 s). The old house was sold in 1948
  to a private family. **Stångehuvud was dark** (extinguished 1940, replaced by Stångholmen).
- **Måseskär's light was white, not red** (fixed red 1865-1887, white flashes after). Electrified 1950.
- **Buoyage was not red-port/green-starboard.** In 1950 Sweden used its own old system: by compass side of the
  fairway, red on the N/E side, black on the S/W side, with broom ("kvast") and ball ("ballong") tops. The
  Nordic system came 1965; IALA System A reached the west coast on **17 March 1980**.
- **Plastic boats came late:** serial GRP Koster yachts 1967, Folkbåt GRP 1976; steel fishing boats from 1959.
- **The 1950 Lysekil-Fiskebäckskil ferry could not be identified.** Only the old passenger boat "Ran" (built
  1876 as Stocka) is tied to the route by a museum record, undated.
- **Sea level:** about 0.13 m higher than today (range 0.12-0.15), our arithmetic on SMHI rates.
- **Population around 1950:** Grundsund 983, Fiskebäckskil 370 (535 with Östersidan), Gullholmen parish 466,
  Hälleviksstrand 277, Käringön 336 (1944), Lysekil ~6,000 (1960 only), Härmanö no count.

Evidence per place (sources in brackets):

- **Lysekil.** Stad since 1 April 1903 [S31]. Lysekil tätort 5,971 on 1 Nov 1960 [S31]; no 1950 number was found.
  Lysekils Mekaniska Verkstad (1899) made the Skandia hot-bulb engines [S30]; hot-bulb engines made in Lysekil
  until 1966 [S53]. WWII brought "en slutgiltig nedgång" for the stone industry, some production stayed
  [S57, S56]. https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysekil
- **Fiskebäckskil.** Counts 1950: 370 excluding Östersidan, 535 including it (SCB) [S32]. Bath resort since 1867
  (museum record, UMFA53264:0208); locals rented rooms to summer guests [S54]. Municipalsamhälle dissolved 31 Dec
  1951 [S32]. Östersidan had guano, saltery and cannery works in the 1800s [S54]. The only cannery name is a
  bibliography title, "AB Lyckes söners konservfabrik på Östersidan" [S32], with no dates.
- **Grundsund.** 983 in 1950 [S33]. "Förvandlades aldrig till badort"; 20th-century raincoat manufacture and
  canning, a Bovik mackerel cannery (undated) [S33]. Home port of the wooden vessel Sandö, long-line fishing from
  1949 [S48]. Canal and harbour built 1914-1918 [S55].
- **Gullholmen.** Parish 466 in 1950, 524 in 1940, peak "drygt 800" in 1910 [S38, S41]. About 150 houses and 100
  boathouses in 1920 [S40]. "Storsjöfisket var grunden i ekonomin ... fram till 1900-talets mitt" and fish
  processing "fram till 1960-talet" [S40, S41]; Gullholmsbaden (Reso, 1942) [S39, S40]. Hallgren cannery from 1864,
  bought 1898 by AB Sveriges Förenade Konservfabriker [S41]; the Länsstyrelsen text in the project file says the
  cannery closed after WWI (no year): **conflict, left null**.
- **Härmanö.** No own count. Old fishing hamlet Härmanösund abandoned 1705 [S40]; feldspar quarried at Höpallen
  and Stenvik in the first half of the 1900s [S36]; reserve only from 1967 [S36].
- **Käringön.** 336 inhabitants in 1944, 548 in 1925 [S35]. Double houses dominate; harbour made in the 1930s;
  pilots and lighthouse staff lived on Lotsberget [S40].
- **Hälleviksstrand.** 277 in 1950, 346 in 1940 [S37]. Boatyard from 1907 (cutter rebuilds; Iceland-fishing
  boats built under roof in the 1940s), cannery "Bovik konservfabriker AB" 1934-1976, steamer pier, bus road
  1927, fire of 1905 [S39, S40].

Not covered: Skaftö hamlets (Stockevik, Bovik, Rågårdsvik). Nothing was read for them.

## C. Sea marks and buoyage

**Switch to IALA A:** SJÖFS 1979:16 (issued 11 June 1979) in force **17 March 1980** for Svinesund-Stenshuvud,
mark by mark as announced in Ufs [S20]; Åhus-Gävle 30 March 1981 [S21]; fyr.org: "början av 1980-talet" [S18].
https://www-n.sjofartsverket.se/globalassets/om-oss/lagrum/sjofs/1970-1989/79-016.pdf

**The 1950 system** [S18, S27, S28]: fyr.org gives "svensk utprickning" until 1965, then "nordisk" 1965 to the
early 1980s, then System A. The only graphic is the 1923 plate (*Svensk Lots* 1923, Bil. 4). That the plate still
held in 1950 is **assumed** (uncertain).

- Fairway, **N and E side: red** (red pole with a broom of twigs on top = kvastprick; red pole with 1-3 slatted
  balls; red cone buoys). **S and W side: black** (plain black-and-white pole = slätprick; black pole with
  balls; black cone buoys).
- Middle of fairway or small shoal passable on both sides: red-and-black banded pole with an X or a ball.
- Open-sea shoals by compass side: N black balls; E black-white with balls; S red with up-pointing brooms;
  W red with down-pointing brooms.
- Wrecks: **green**. Light buoys red or black with a lantern cage; bell and sound buoys.
- Fixed marks: stone cairns (Kl, sometimes whitewashed), pole marks, leading marks/boards and white painted
  patches [S24, S25, S26].
- **No yellow marks, no green lateral marks.** The 1965 Nordic rules [S19] differ and are NOT 1950.

Today's kinds (assets/coast/seamarks.json, OSM, 226 marks = 206 real + 20 invented harbour gates):

| Kind today | Count | In 1950? | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| lateral (53 red, 47 green) | 100 | partly | black/red by compass side, brooms and balls; no green |
| cardinal | 10 | partly | other colours (N black, E black-white, S red, W red) |
| isolated danger (black piles) | 38 | partly | red-black banded "kryssprick", fixed poles on rocks |
| special (yellow) | 37 | **no** | do not draw (or draw as plain black/red) |
| light (minor lights) | 41 | partly | AGA lights from 1883-1949 existed at many sites; each not checked |
| leading lines | 12 | unknown | the kind existed; these 12 not dated |

Do not invent positions: use today's positions, change the look.

## D. Boats

- **Large fishing boat.** Sandö, LL 158, built 1948 (Rönnäng, Tjörn): 24.6 x 6.6 m, carvel oak; red copper
  bottom, **white linseed-oil paint above the waterline, a red stripe, black registration**; 225 hp June
  Munktell (1948), B&W Alpha 1955 [S48]. Wooden carvel boats with a "rund bullig form" so they pack tightly in
  the harbours; steel boats from 1959 [S49].
- **Engines.** Hot-bulb ("tändkule/råoljemotor") two-stroke, the "dunk-dunk"; larger Bohuslän boats often had
  two cylinders [S52]; Skandia engines made in Lysekil until 1966 [S53]. "Tuff-tuff" as a word was not found.
- **Cutters.** English-built wooden cutters (Gullholmen: 57 of 70 sailing vessels in 1913, with names such as
  Conquest and Liberator [S41]); 17 on Käringön in 1915 [S40]. 1950 numbers not found.
- **Snipa and Koster.** Snipa: pointed both ends, 4-8 m, open motor snipa for nets [S50]; Koster: clinker,
  gaff-rigged decked drift-net boat 10-12 m, K-class yachts from 1934, K6 1944 [S50].
- **Leisure.** Folkbåt: 7.64 m, clinker wood, launched 1942, c.400 by 1952 [S51]; skärgårdskryssare rule 1908.
  **Plastic:** serial GRP Kosterbåtar from 1967 [S50], Folkbåt GRP 1976 [S51].
- **Not found:** fleet sizes in 1950; hull colours of ordinary fishing boats; "hajk" and "storbåt" as fishing
  boat types (they mean scout hike and ship's launch).
- **THE FERRY.** *Not identified.* Operator, name, size, colours for 1950: not found. What exists: a Bohusläns
  museum postcard of the passenger ferry **Ran** in Fiskebäckskil harbour (undated; "byggd 1876 vid Hudiksvalls
  mekaniska verkstad som Stocka"; presumed photographer Träff, d. 1947) [S44, S45]; a 1959 postcard of the
  passenger boat **Gullmaren** from Oxevik at Lysekil [S46]. Do not confuse with the later M/S Ran (1945 navy tug;
  Lysekil passenger boat 1963-75) [S47], Gullmarsfärjan II (1965-91, search summary) [S59], Carl Wilhelmson
  (1990) [S58].
- **Other passenger boats.** S/S Bohuslän (1914, 43.13 m, 280 passengers, 12 kn, Göteborg-Marstrand-Lysekil-
  Smögen-Gravarne, ~40 years) [S43]; coastal steamers black and yellow, white mostly from the 1920s-30s, the
  steamer era ended in the 1950s [S42]. Names of boats to Gullholmen and Käringön in 1950: not found.

## E. A 1950 fishing village

- **Houses.** One-and-a-half-storey wooden double houses ("dubbelhus") with vertical panel, symmetric windows,
  protruding eaves and shared carpentry details; smaller single-width cottages around the oldest harbours; a
  whitewashed stone cottage on Gullholmen [S40, S41]. Dense, gables to the west on Käringön and Hälleviksstrand.
- **Colours.** Present-day municipal guidance describing traditional practice: dwellings white or mild light,
  window frames green, English red or ochre (not white), blue "inte vanligt förrän från 1900-talets mitt";
  boathouses red, occasionally ochre, or unpainted; roofs red unglazed clay tile [S40]. **This is a modern
  preservation rule, not a 1950 survey** (uncertain). A search summary of Bohusläns museum agrees: light oil
  paint on dwellings, red slamfärg on outbuildings [S60, weak].
- **Boathouses and piers.** Rows of sjöbodar and magasin (Gullholmen about 100 in 1920); long pier with
  boathouses along Grundsund's canal; steamer quays at Gullholmen and Hälleviksstrand [S40, S41, S54]. Net racks
  and fish-drying racks: not found.
- **Canning factories** (see the JSON): Gullholmen (Hallgren, conflict on 1950), Hälleviksstrand (Bovik
  konservfabriker AB 1934-1976, operating), Lysekil Sjöbol (undated), Fiskebäckskil Östersidan (AB Lyckes söner,
  bibliography title only), Grundsund (Bovik, undated). None of the buildings' looks (brick or wood, chimney,
  size) was found.
- **Summer houses.** Lysekil: Curman's villas (1800s), bathing establishment burned 1939; Fiskebäckskil: bath
  resort 1867, bath restaurant; Hälleviksstrand and Käringön: guests of the 1880s-90s later built summer houses;
  Gullholmen: Gullholmsbaden 1942. Paid leave two weeks 1938, three weeks 1951 [S39]. "Snickarglädje" was not
  found as a term.
- **Grundsund canal.** Built January 1914 to 1918, state aid SEK 650,000 (1913), canal through the village;
  no formal opening until 2018 [S55, S54]. Width and depth: **not found**.

## F. Water level

Rates (SMHI, re-read in this mission [S29]): Marstrand 0.16, Smögen 0.18, Kungsvik 0.20 cm/yr. 75 years (1950
to 2025) x 0.16-0.20 = **12-15 cm** (mid 0.18 x 75 = 13.5). `offsetM = 0.13`, `rangeM = [0.12, 0.15]`.
Our arithmetic, not a published figure; the gauge era overlaps these 75 years, so the range is narrow. Effect on
"awash" rocks: about 13 cm lower in 1950, small but not zero.
