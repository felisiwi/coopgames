# West Orust: the start area, as story material

Dated events, people, wrecks, cairns, signal fires and legends for the islands and villages round the
game's start area: Käringön, Måseskär, Gullholmen, Härmanö, Hälleviksstrand, Mollösund and the
"Kråkesund" gap. Raw material for writing, not finished text.

**Rules this file keeps**
- Nothing is from memory. Every item below was read on the page it links to.
- A century or decade is never turned into a year: it is written "1700s (approx.)".
- Legends are marked as legends, have no date, and say who tells them.
- Where a page gave no verbatim sentence to the research tool (only a summary), the item says "as relayed".
- If something wanted was not found, it is listed under **Not found**, not filled in.

**Confidence:** *high* = a museum, heritage-authority, church or nature-reserve source stated it and the wording was read
(or it was read in the museum's own report); *medium* = sv.wikipedia or a regional tourism page; *low* = a search snippet, a
paraphrase we could not confirm, or an undated tourism mention. The population and settlement numbers behind the map live in
`settlements-history.json` (see its `unsourced` list); this file is the narrative layer.

Item format: **when** — what happened. *(source · confidence)*

## Käringön

### Dated events
- **1659** — The island is written "Kierlingøon". *([sv.wikipedia Käringön](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Käringön) · confidence: medium)*
- **1700s (approx.)** — Käringön "was put on the map" by the great herring period. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/karingon/) · confidence: medium)*
- **19 November 1795** — Käringön's parish is formed as a chapel parish, split from Morlanda. *([sv.wikipedia Käringöns socken](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Käringöns_socken) · confidence: medium)*
- **1796** — The church is taken into use; about 300 islanders now have their own. The island is said to have been inhabited since the 1500s. Communion silver is given by "Fem rederliga män på Käringön" (five respectable men of Käringön). *([Svenska kyrkan, Käringöns kyrka](https://www.svenskakyrkan.se/orust/karingons-kyrka) · confidence: high, as relayed)*
- **1797** — A bell from 1797, cast by Gerhard Horner and donated by a fishing company on the island, hangs in the tower (the page names the year of the bell; it does not say when it was hung). The first person buried is Cecilia Olofsdotter, born 1715. *([Svenska kyrkan, Käringöns kyrka](https://www.svenskakyrkan.se/orust/karingons-kyrka) · confidence: high, as relayed)*
- **29 September 1886** — Käringön's municipalsamhälle (a local government for the built-up village) is set up; it is dissolved 31 December 1959. *([sv.wikipedia Käringön](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Käringön) · confidence: medium)*
- **Ascension weekend 1993** — Memorial plaques in the church, honouring islanders lost at sea, are dedicated. (An anchor stone outside is also mentioned; the dedication sentence may cover only the plaques.) *([Svenska kyrkan, Käringöns kyrka](https://www.svenskakyrkan.se/orust/karingons-kyrka) · confidence: high, as relayed)*

### People
- **Olof Knape (later Strömstierna), born 29 January 1664 on Käringön.** Went to sea in 1672, was in the East Indies until 1697 (the page gives no span of Dutch East India Company service), received his commission as admiralty captain in 1700, was ennobled as Strömstierna (1715, read from the page's words "samma år"; the one year here that is inferred) and died 17 September 1730 at Vese säteri. A second source also says he was born on Käringön in 1664, went to sea at eight and was appointed by Karl XII in 1700, and that the name Strömstierna appears at several places on the island. *([sv.wikipedia Olof Strömstierna](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olof_Strömstierna), [vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/karingon/) · confidence: medium)*

### Cairns, signal fires, sea marks
- **Undated** — The name may contain "käring" in the sense of a whitewashed cairn used as a sea mark ("(vitmålat) röse till ledning för sjöfarande"), per Svenskt ortnamnslexikon (2003) as cited by Wikipedia; the tourism page adds that the word "käring" also named a navigation mark at Saltskär, near the island, and says the origin of the name is disputed. *([sv.wikipedia Käringön](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Käringön), [vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/karingon/) · confidence: medium, a name theory, not a fact)*

### Legends and sayings
- **Legend (undated), told by a regional tourism site** — Fishermen saw women aboard as bad luck and divided them into lucky "turkäringar" and unlucky "oturskäringar"; meeting an unlucky one on the way to the boat meant turning back. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/karingon/) · confidence: medium, as relayed)*

### Undated facts
- A former pilot dwelling, Lotsutkiken, about 10 m², stands at the island's south-western tip, and the Swedish Sea Rescue Society runs one of Sweden's largest rescue stations on the island. No dates given. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/karingon/) · confidence: low)*

### Not found
Shipwrecks near Käringön before 1900 (ship, year, what happened); founding dates of the pilot station and the rescue station; who built the church; the school and the shop; any Bronze Age remains or vårdkase (signal-fire) site on the island (the parish article says only that "tomtningar" — house foundations — have been found); ghost stories beyond the "käring" sayings.

## Måseskär (the lighthouse off Käringön)

### Dated events
- **Before 1829** — A stone cairn (stenkummel) stands on the spot. *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*
- **1829** — A pole beacon (stångmärke) is built at the Crown's expense where the cairn stood. *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*
- **1864–1865** — The plans are approved in 1864; the lighthouse, designed by Gustaf von Heidenstam, is built in 1865 with the keepers' buildings and lit on 8 October; the old beacon is taken down. *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*
- **1869** — The tower is painted with white fields so it can be told from the neighbouring lights by day. *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*
- **1873** — Two signal cannons on carriages are installed for fog. A coal-fired steam fog siren replaces them in the mid-1880s, in a new engine house. *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*
- **1950, 1955, 1978** — The light is electrified (1950); an electric motor replaces the clockwork (1955); in 1978 a new white plastic lighthouse is built beside the old one, the old light is put out in August and fog signalling is automated. *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*
- **1988 and 7 December 2017** — Stiftelsen Måseskär takes over use of the old lighthouse, house and outbuilding (1988); the site is declared a state building monument (2017, as relayed). *([sv.wikipedia Måseskär](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Måseskär) · confidence: medium)*

### Wrecks (weak, snippet-level; do not quote as fact)
- **Skytteren** — a wreck said to lie near Måseskär, about 75 m deep and 11 km west of Orust, leaking oil since 1942. Only a search summary; the page itself (vrak.se) returned 404 and 1942 is not confirmed as the sinking year. *([vrak.se](https://vrak.se/en/wrecks-and-remains/skytteren) · confidence: low)*
- **After the Second World War (approx.)** — the Allies are said to have sunk about 20 ships west of Måseskär at roughly 200 m depth; raised arsenic levels are suspected to come from chemical weapons. Dates and ship names were not found. *([Havs- och vattenmyndigheten press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/havochvatten/pressreleases/ny-studie-visar-spaar-av-kemiska-stridsmedel-i-fisk-och-skaldjur-vid-maaseskaersvraken-1797193) · confidence: low)*

### Not found
Names of the lighthouse keepers (the Wikipedia article names none); pre-1900 wrecks.

## Gullholmen

### Dated events
- **1253** — The Norse saga of King Håkon Håkonsson says he put in at Hermanösund in his 36th regnal year, which the museum report dates to 1253. Another passage of the same saga, written in the 1260s, says Håkon had "Gullö (Gulleyna), Marstrand and many other desolate islands in Viken" built up; several historians have equated Gulleyna with Gullholmen, others doubt it, and where Hermanösund lay is unclear, so there is no secure medieval Gullholmen. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **Middle Ages (approx.)** — Two medieval potsherds from test pits show that the sound between Gullholmen and Lilla Härmanö was used as a harbour. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1565** — King Fredrik II writes to the Lübeck admiral Fredrik Knevel allowing him to build two booths, one in Hermanösund and one in Mollösund. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high; the report treats Hermanösund as the sound between Lilla Härmanö and Gullholmen, a placement it calls disputed)*
- **1572** — One of the king's customs officers is posted at Hermanösund. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1594** — The Aalborg town accounts for 1583–1599 record that two men from Gullholmen, Hans Persen and Niels Sørensen, paid "bropenge" (harbour or bridge dues) when they visited the town with their ships. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1587** — Gullholmen is listed as the home port of ships in the Öresund toll accounts. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1588** — The oldest secure evidence of settlement: a letter mentioning Jens Opekop "paa Guldholmen". *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1610 and 1645** — The 1610 extra-tax roll lists 24 resident strandsittare (shore-dwellers) on Gullholmen; by 1645 there are about 39 households, plus some "forarmede" (impoverished). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **Late 1600s** — About 20 fishing families live on the island (citing Holmberg 1979). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1673** — Kettil Classon Felterus's map of Bohuslän shows Gullholmen and Stora and Lilla Hermanö (Figure 5 of the report; original in Riksarkivet). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1700s to about 1800 (approx.)** — After the great herring period the fishing village expands strongly; around 1800 there are about a hundred dwelling houses. When the herring left, building slowed, and it picked up again in the 1870s with the next great herring period. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)* A cannery, a saltery and a sailmaker's arrived in the herring periods of the 1700s and 1800s. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/sodrabohuslan/produkter/gullholmen-och-harmano/) · confidence: medium)*
- **1798–1799** — Permission is given to form a separate chapel parish out of Morlanda; Captain Abraham Gustaf Bildt of Morlanda säteri helps obtain timber; the church is finished in autumn 1799 (it stands on Lilla Härmanö). The tower is replaced in 1904 (as relayed); the altarpiece dates from 1828. *([Svenska kyrkan, Gullholmens kyrka](https://www.svenskakyrkan.se/orust/gullholmens-kyrka) · confidence: high, as relayed)*
- **29 January 1886** — Gullholmen's municipalsamhälle is established; it is dissolved 31 December 1959. *([sv.wikipedia Gullholmen](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullholmen) · confidence: medium)*
- **After the First World War** — Fishing pays less, people move away, many houses become summer homes, and the school and the cannery close. *([Länsstyrelsen Västra Götaland, Härmanö](https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/vastra-gotaland/besok-och-upptack/naturreservat/harmano.html) · confidence: high; the page gives no year)*
- **Until 1999** — Gullholmen is a "kronoholme", state-owned land. How that ended was not found. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/sodrabohuslan/produkter/gullholmen-och-harmano/) · confidence: medium)*

### Wrecks
- **The harbour wreck (found 1936; name unknown).** When Gullholmen's harbour was deepened in 1936, the remains of a larger wooden ship were found in its north part (ancient-monument record Gullholmen 25). The Gothenburg maritime museum's director, Olof Traung, dated it to the mid-1600s from how it was built. The museum's 2012 report dates the timber: probably felled between 1727 and 1827, so the ship sank some time from the 1730s to the mid-1800s, and "both Traung's 1600s dating and the link to Tordenskiold claimed in folk tradition can be dismissed". Oak, carvel-built, about 13 × 8 m remaining. *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*

### Cairns and remains
- **Undated** — A now-vanished offering cairn ("offerkast", ancient-monument record Gullholmen 15) is recorded; a stone with initials and years from the 1700s and 1800s survives (Gullholmen 18). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*

### Legends
- **Legend (as told; the Great Northern War, 1700–1721), told locally** — The wreck in the harbour is said to be a ship from the Danish admiral Peder Wessel Tordenskiold's fleet, lost in the Great Northern War. The museum rejects this after dating the timber (see above). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high that the tradition exists and that the museum rejects it)*

### Not found
Vårdkase (signal-fire) sites; an accepted origin of the name; how the crown-island status ended; dates for the saltery and cannery; named shipwrecks other than the unnamed harbour wreck; notable people (only Captain Bildt of Morlanda, who helped with the church timber, was named).

## Härmanö

### Dated events
- **1500s and 1600s (approx.)** — About ten house platforms ("tomtningar") from the 1500s and 1600s have been excavated at Skållehus near the island's southern tip; some houses were probably lived in all year. The settlers fished cod and other whitefish and hunted dolphin, seal and seabirds; bones of cattle, sheep, pigs, geese and hens were found. *([Västkuststiftelsen, Härmanö folder](https://vastkuststiftelsen.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/harmano_naturreservat_karta_folder.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **From at least the 1500s (approx.)** — Farm settlement on the island's east side, combined with home fishing and freight sailing; the farm Härm, in the middle of the east shore, is the oldest and all buildings were once there. *([Västkuststiftelsen, Härmanö folder](https://vastkuststiftelsen.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/harmano_naturreservat_karta_folder.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1673** — Felterus's map of Bohuslän names Stora and Lilla Hermanö (Figure 5 of the report; original in Riksarkivet). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1831** — At the laga skifte (land reform) the island's farms are divided into five parts; the stone walls that still cross the landscape were built then. *([Västkuststiftelsen, Härmanö folder](https://vastkuststiftelsen.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/harmano_naturreservat_karta_folder.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **First half of the 1900s (approx.)** — Feldspar is quarried at Höpallen and Stenvik. *([Västkuststiftelsen, Härmanö folder](https://vastkuststiftelsen.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/harmano_naturreservat_karta_folder.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **2000 and 2012** — In 2000, 138 people live on Härmanö (118 of them in the built-up area) and 20 on the island of Gullholmen (together the parish total of 158); in 2012 only 10 permanent residents are left on Gullholmen and 92 on Härmanö. *([sv.wikipedia Gullholmen](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullholmen) · confidence: medium)*
- **1967** — Härmanö nature reserve is established (the infobox gives a day in October; a search summary gave a different day, so only the year is used). *([sv.wikipedia Härmanö](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Härmanö) · confidence: medium)*

### Names and shape
- The reserve "consists of Stora and Lilla Härmanö"; the island is six kilometres long and exposed to wind and weather, with bare rock, barren flats and heath in the west and the farmland on the east side. Härmanö and the island of Gullholmen together form the community of Gullholmen, "originally a fishing village". *([Västkuststiftelsen, Härmanö folder](https://vastkuststiftelsen.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/harmano_naturreservat_karta_folder.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- The medieval mentions of "Hermanösund" (1253, 1565, 1572, 1587) are of the sound, not certainly of the island, and where the sound lay is disputed (see Gullholmen). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*

### Not found
Cairns or signal-fire sites on Härmanö; herring, decline or resort years for Härmanö itself; named wrecks, people or legends.

## Hälleviksstrand

### Dated events
- **1617** — First mention in written sources. Known locally as "Strana". It lies by the bay Lerkilen; no page explains the origin of the name. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/halleviksstrand/) · confidence: medium)*
- **1700s (approx.)** — In the herring period the village flourishes with herring salting. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/halleviksstrand/) · confidence: medium)*
- **Late 1800s (approx.)** — In the bathing-resort era, warm-bath houses and pensions (guesthouses) are built for well-off summer guests. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/halleviksstrand/) · confidence: medium)*
- **1904** — The church is built ("uppförd år 1904"). *([sv.wikipedia Hälleviksstrand](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hälleviksstrand) · confidence: medium)*
- **1905** — A large fire destroys much of the village; a shipyard, a cannery and a stonemason's yard start at the same time. *([vastsverige.com](https://www.vastsverige.com/orust/produkter/halleviksstrand/) · confidence: medium)*

People, wrecks, cairns, signal fires and legends tied to Hälleviksstrand; the origin of its name.

## Mollösund

### Dated events
- **1345–1365 (the ship), 1981 (the find)** — A medieval cog (a trading ship) is recovered off Mollösund in 1981; it lay in Östra leran. Dendrochronology gives a build year of 1345–1365, with the oak felled around 1365, from at least two areas, two samples probably from Funen and Zealand in Denmark. *([DigitaltMuseum, Kogg, del av skrov](https://digitaltmuseum.se/021028784979/kogg-del-av-skrov) · confidence: high)*
- **1500s (approx.)** — Mollösund is then part of Denmark-Norway and a hub of the profitable herring fishery; one of Sweden's oldest fishing villages. *([sv.wikipedia Mollösund](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollösund) · confidence: medium)*
- **1556–1588** — The first church was probably built during the great herring-fishing period of these years. *([Svenska kyrkan, Mollösunds kyrka](https://svenskakyrkan.se/orust/mollosunds-kyrka) · confidence: high)*
- **1565** — King Fredrik II lets the Lübeck admiral Fredrik Knevel build a booth in Mollösund (and one in Hermanösund). *([Bohusläns museum rapport 2012:39](https://www.bohuslansmuseum.se/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/BM_Rapport_2012-39.pdf) · confidence: high)*
- **1750s (approx.)** — The herring returns and the village prospers again; the population rises to 500. *([sv.wikipedia Mollösund](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollösund) · confidence: medium)*
- **26 June 1866** — The new church is consecrated, designed by the city architect H. J. Strömberg. *([Svenska kyrkan, Mollösunds kyrka](https://svenskakyrkan.se/orust/mollosunds-kyrka) · confidence: high)*
- **29 January 1886** — Mollösund municipalsamhälle is established. *([sv.wikipedia Mollösund](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollösund) · confidence: medium)*

### Not found
Named people; wrecks other than the cog; cairns and signal fires; legends; harbour history in detail.

## Cairns, signal fires (vårdkasar) and the sea marks of west Orust

What *was* found: a cairn on the Måseskär rock that the 1829 pole beacon replaced; the possible "whitewashed cairn" meaning of the name Käringön, with a second "käring" sea mark at Saltskär; a vanished offering cairn on Gullholmen. What was **not** found, after searches on all the places above: any vårdkase (signal-fire) site or system on Orust with a source, and any Bronze Age cairn (kummel/röse) record. Riksantikvarieämbetet's ancient-monument register (fornsok.se) was not reachable by the tools, and is the place to look next.

## Threads worth pulling (my suggestions, not facts)

- The **harbour wreck vs the Tordenskiold legend** at Gullholmen: a story people tell, and the proof that it cannot be true.
- **Olof Knape**, born on a rock island in 1664, rising through Dutch and Swedish service: a native son worth a name on a quay.
- **The cairn that became a lighthouse**: Måseskär's stone cairn, then a pole beacon (1829), then the iron lighthouse (1865).
- **Hermanösund**: a sound that appears in a saga (1253), in a royal letter (1565) and in toll accounts (1587), whose location historians still argue about.
- The **medieval cog** off Mollösund: a Danish-built trading ship, found in the same waters the player sails.
